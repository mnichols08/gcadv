Simon Pearce is one of the more unusual stops near Oakland.

It is not a museum exhibit or a staged attraction built only for visitors. It is a working glass facility where people can watch glassmakers shape molten glass from a viewing area above the production floor.

That makes it a useful stop for a Garrett County day that needs something quieter, indoors, and different from the usual lake, trail, waterfall, and mountain itinerary.

The official Simon Pearce location refers to this as Oakland. You may still see older or local references that place it around Mountain Lake Park, but for visitor purposes the current Oakland listing is the clearest way to think about it.

## This is a working glass factory

{% photo "[PHOTO: visitor catwalk]", "Photo placeholder for the visitor viewing area above the glassblowing workshop.", "wide", "simon" %}

The important thing to understand is that visitors are watching real production.

Glassblowers are not simply performing a short demonstration because people happen to be standing there. The Garrett County facility is part of Simon Pearce's glassmaking operation, and the viewing area lets visitors look down onto that work from above.

That changes the feeling of the stop. You are not being walked through a scripted attraction. You are watching a craft process that has its own pace, repetition, teamwork, and heat.

Current official visitor information has listed glassblowing viewing at roughly 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. daily, while the store remains open longer. If watching the glassblowers is the reason you are going, check the current Simon Pearce Oakland page before driving over.

Store open does not automatically mean glassblowers working.

## Watching the glassblowers

{% photo "[PHOTO: glassblower at work]", "Photo placeholder for a Simon Pearce glassmaker working with molten glass.", "", "simon" %}

The process is worth seeing because it is both physical and precise.

A piece begins as hot glass gathered from the furnace. From there, the work depends on movement, timing, tools, breath, gravity, and judgment. The makers turn the glass, shape it, reheat it, inspect it, and keep adjusting before the object becomes recognizable.

From the viewing area, you can watch an orange gather of glass gradually become something more familiar: a bowl, a glass, or another finished form.

It is also a good reminder that handmade objects are not abstract. Someone stood near heat, handled tools, made decisions, and worked with a material that does not wait politely while you think.

## The building has another life behind it

Simon Pearce is often associated with Vermont and New England, which makes the Garrett County location a little surprising at first.

The Oakland-area facility occupies a former industrial/manufacturing building associated with Bausch & Lomb. Simon Pearce later adapted the building for glass production and shipping.

That detail is part of what makes the stop interesting. This is not a polished tourism village built from scratch. It is a working company using an older industrial space in western Maryland to make high-end handmade glass.

Keep the history in that simple shape. The building had an earlier manufacturing life, and now it has another one.

## The factory store

{% photo "[PHOTO: factory store / Seconds]", "Photo placeholder for the factory store, Seconds area or finished handmade glass.", "", "simon" %}

There is a store on site, and it sells Simon Pearce pieces.

You can expect first-quality glass and other Simon Pearce products, and the store has historically included Seconds: pieces that do not meet first-quality standards but may still be attractive or useful. Limited experimental or R&D pieces may also appear.

Inventory changes, so this is not a promise about exactly what will be there when you visit.

The store is most interesting after you have watched the glassblowing. A finished glass or bowl reads differently once you have seen the heat, movement, and handling behind it.

You do not have to buy anything for the stop to be worthwhile. The making is the point.

## Make it part of an Oakland afternoon

Simon Pearce works best as part of a larger Oakland-area day.

You can watch glassblowing, spend some time in the factory store, and then continue into Oakland for the B&O station, museums, downtown wandering, books, records, antiques, or a slower lunch.

That makes the stop more useful than treating it as the only reason to leave Deep Creek Lake.

## Know before you go

As of the last check for this article, Simon Pearce's official Oakland visitor information listed glassblowing viewing roughly daily from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m.

The store has listed longer hours than the viewing period.

Schedules can change. If glassblowing is the main reason for your visit, check the current official Simon Pearce Oakland page before leaving.

The facility is listed at 265 Glass Drive, Oakland, Maryland.

Again, do not assume that store hours and glassblowing viewing hours are the same thing.

## Is it worth stopping?

Yes, especially if you like watching how things are made.

Simon Pearce is a good fit if you want a short indoor stop, are already spending time around Oakland, need something different from the lake-and-trail itinerary, or are traveling with someone who enjoys craft, design, or production processes.

If you have no interest in glassmaking or browsing the store, it may be a short stop rather than a major destination.

That is fine. Not every Garrett County side trip needs to fill half a day.

This one is memorable because it shows a different kind of local work: careful, hot, repetitive, skilled, and easy to overlook if you only think of Garrett County as a lake vacation.
